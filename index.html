Case study 01

Global Human Resource Management System (HRMS) Implementation

Optimising fragmented HR systems and disconnected employee data across global operations.

Type
Professional engagement
My role
Business Analyst
Focus
Stakeholder engagement, requirements elicitation and analysis

01 The problem

HR systems were fragmented, employee data was disconnected and HR processes were inconsistent across the organisation’s global operations. This affected efficiency, decision-making, employee experience and workforce management.

  • Fragmented employee dataRecords spread across multiple systems, with duplicates, inconsistencies and no central global database.
  • Inefficient HR operationsOnboarding, performance reviews and admin relied on manual or semi-automated steps, causing delays and errors.
  • Limited global reportingInconsistent data made accurate workforce reporting and strategic decisions difficult.
  • Inconsistent employee experienceDifferent locations followed different HR processes.
  • Compliance and riskVaried employment, labour and data protection rules added complexity and risk.
  • High IT maintenance costsMultiple disconnected platforms demanded significant IT resource and spend.

02 What I did

  1. Understood the organisation. Clarified its structure, objectives and the specific problem to be solved.
  2. Mapped stakeholders. Reviewed the organisational chart, engaged relevant departments and built a Stakeholder Matrix rating influence and interest.
  3. Defined roles and communication. Created a RACI Matrix and a Communication Plan covering channels and frequency, based on stakeholder preferences.
  4. Elicited and analysed requirements. Gathered and documented requirements through interviews, workshops and reviews of existing processes.
  5. Prioritised and validated. Confirmed requirements with stakeholders before solution design and development.

03 Tools and approach

  • Miro
  • Figma
  • Mockflow
  • Workshops
  • Interviews & surveys
  • Process mapping

04 Outcome and learning

The HR system optimisation gave the organisation improved HR processes, better data management and more efficient handling of employee information. It also exposed gaps and inefficiencies in existing processes, helping the organisation improve workflows and make better-informed decisions.

  • Improved efficiency in HR operations and administrative processes
  • Better organisation, accessibility and accuracy of employee records
  • Reduced reliance on manual processes
  • Improved HR workflow and information management
  • Better alignment between the HR system and business requirements
  • Stronger collaboration between HR stakeholders and the technical team

What I learned: effective stakeholder engagement, clear requirements documentation, prioritisation and continuous collaboration between technical teams and business users are what make a solution fit organisational needs.

Case study 02

Online Appointment Booking Portal

Replacing a manual, paper-based appointment process with a centralised digital system across service centres.

Type
Professional engagement
My role
Business Analyst, from scoping through to post-implementation review
Focus
Process mapping, requirements prioritisation and User Acceptance Testing

01 The problem

Service centres relied on walk-in visits, handwritten customer records and manual queue management. The result was long queues, extended waiting times, missed appointments and inefficient use of staff. Customers also lacked information on availability, waiting times and required documents before visiting. With demand rising, the process was becoming unsustainable.

Objective: analyse, design and implement a centralised, automated appointment management system. It aimed to:

  • Automate schedulingBook, reschedule and cancel online without visiting a centre.
  • Improve customer experienceClear information on availability, waiting times and documents needed.
  • Reduce waiting and queuesA digital queue process to ease overcrowding.
  • Improve efficiencyLess manual admin so staff can focus on service.
  • Minimise missed appointmentsConfirmations, email or SMS reminders and cancellation notices.
  • Allocate resources betterVisibility of volumes and peak periods, with a scalable base for growth.

02 What I did

  1. Scoped the problem. Understood objectives and scope, identified stakeholders, and produced a stakeholder matrix, RACI matrix and communication plan.
  2. Mapped the current state. Ran stakeholder interviews and workshops, documented the As-Is process, identified bottlenecks and carried out gap analysis.
  3. Defined requirements. Gathered, documented and prioritised requirements using MoSCoW, then validated them with stakeholders.
  4. Designed the future state. Developed To-Be process maps and worked with the technical team so the solution met the identified needs.
  5. Bridged business and technology. Clarified requirements and managed changes during development.
  6. Supported testing. Wrote test scenarios, coordinated UAT with business users, documented defects and followed issues to resolution.
  7. Supported go-live. Helped with implementation, user training and the move from the manual process to the digital system.
  8. Reviewed results. Took part in the post-implementation review, gathered user feedback, assessed the objectives and documented lessons learned.

03 Tools and approach

  • Microsoft Word, Excel, Visio & PowerPoint
  • Jira
  • Figma
  • Stakeholder analysis
  • Requirements elicitation
  • As-Is / To-Be process mapping
  • Gap analysis
  • MoSCoW prioritisation
  • UAT

04 Outcome and learning

A digital customer appointment management system was successfully implemented, improving on the manual process. Customers gained a more convenient way to book, reschedule and cancel, while automated notifications helped reduce missed appointments. Scheduling improved, administrative workload fell and management gained better visibility of customer demand and operational performance.

What I learned: understand the business problem before recommending a solution, and make sure requirements are clearly documented and validated. I also built experience in process mapping, requirements prioritisation, working with technical teams and supporting UAT. The role is about identifying improvement opportunities and making sure the final solution delivers genuine business value.
